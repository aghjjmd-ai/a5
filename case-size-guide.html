The Case Size Guide

A watch that fits looks deliberate. Here is how to choose diameter, lug-to-lug length, thickness and strap width for your own wrist.

01Measure your wrist

Wrap a strip of paper around your wrist just above the wrist bone, mark where it overlaps and measure the length with a ruler. Also measure the flat top of your wrist from side to side; this is the space a watch actually sits on, and it is usually 5 to 6 cm on an average wrist.

02Diameter by wrist size

WristComfortable diameterNotes
Under 15 cm32–37 mmDress watches and smaller sports models
15–16.5 cm34–39 mmMost classic sizes
16.5–18 cm38–41 mmThe modern middle ground
18–19.5 cm40–44 mmLarger sports and dive watches
Over 19.5 cm42 mm and upWatch thickness matters too

These are starting points, not rules. Bezel style changes how big a watch looks: a thin bezel and a large dial wear bigger than the number suggests.

03Lug-to-lug matters most

If the lugs extend past the flat top of your wrist, the watch overhangs and looks too large, even at a modest diameter. A useful rule: keep lug-to-lug length at or below the width of the flat top of your wrist. Lugs that curve down help a longer watch sit better.

04Thickness and shirt cuffs

  • Under 10 mm: slides easily under a shirt cuff.
  • 10–13 mm: typical for automatic sports watches.
  • Over 13 mm: tool and dive watches; noticeable under a fitted sleeve.

05Strap width

Strap width is measured between the lugs. As a guide, it is roughly half the case diameter: 18 mm for a 36 mm case, 20 mm for 38–40 mm, 22 mm for 42 mm and above. A strap that tapers towards the buckle looks more refined on smaller wrists.

06Try before you decide

Print a paper circle at the exact diameter and lug length, or use our Wrist Fit Checker. It takes a minute and saves the disappointment of a watch that never feels right.
